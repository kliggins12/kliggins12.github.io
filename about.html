What Batchmate does

Batchmate is a free analysis tool for Texas Lottery players. It downloads the official historical draw results for every Texas draw game, then generates rolling ticket batches and ranks every possible combination by how it has behaved across the full record — win streaks, droughts, and the closest it has ever come to matching a real draw.

The goal is simple: instead of staring at 1.3 million All or Nothing combinations or thousands of Cash Five lines, the tool surfaces a small, explainable set of "tightest" tickets each day — picks built from recent frequency patterns, absent numbers, and odd/even structure, each labeled with the rules and window size that produced it.

How the tightest tickets are built

For All or Nothing — the flagship game — every ticket is a canonical half of the 24-number pool: since a draw and its complement are equivalent, only combinations containing the number 1 need to be tracked (1,352,078 of them). The generator applies an explicit rule engine to a sliding window of recent draws:

  • Absent anchors — numbers that have not appeared anywhere in the window are kept, on the theory that coverage gaps are the most distinctive signal available.
  • Frequency bands — when every number has appeared, the tool keeps a band of numbers sitting at a similar draw-count level and fills the rest from hot and warm numbers.
  • Parity signatures — the 1–24 pool splits evenly into 12 odd and 12 even numbers, and common draw signatures like 7-odd/5-even are preserved or mirrored deliberately.
  • Auto window sizing — the engine scans window sizes from large to small and keeps whichever depth scores best for each draw session, labeling every ticket with its window (for example night W=6).

The other games use the same rolling philosophy: recent-window frequency bands, rare-number anchors, and box matching for the digit games. Powerball and Mega Millions use era-aware filtering so only draws under the current number matrix are counted.

Reading the table

  • Best match — the closest this exact ticket has ever come to a real draw (11/12 on All or Nothing is the $500 tier; 4/5+B means four mains plus the bonus on Texas Two Step or Powerball).
  • Best on — the most recent date that best match occurred.
  • Data thru — the latest official draw included in the analysis.
  • Source / stats — which rule, window, and historical counts produced the ticket.

The collapsed "Streak analysis" section at the bottom ranks every possible combination by its longest historical win streak and current drought — useful for browsing, but remember it describes the past.

The honest math

Lottery draws are independent random events. No historical pattern — not a streak, a drought, a frequency band, or a ticket that once matched 11 of 12 — changes the probability of the next draw. Batchmate is a descriptive tool: it shows you which tickets have structurally "stuck" to the record and gives you an organized way to choose numbers. It is not a prediction system, and no ticket it generates is more likely to win than any other ticket costing the same price. We say this plainly because you deserve honest tools.

If you choose to play, set a budget, stick to it, and treat it as entertainment. If gambling stops being fun, the Texas Lottery and national resources like the National Council on Problem Gambling (1-800-522-4700) can help.
